Selected work

Products I took from a problem I had personally to something running, plus one full product teardown. Open any of them for the breakdown, down to the metric that would tell me I was wrong.

Target userwho it's for

People who need good photos of themselves and have no photographer — solo creators, profile builders, small-business owners shooting their own marketing. The trigger is specific: a photo needed this week, and nobody to help take it.

Pain pointwhat hurts

Posing is a skill with no feedback loop. You take 40 selfies, hate 39, and can't say why the bad ones are bad. In their words: "I never know what to do with my hands, and by the time I check the photo, the moment is gone." The workaround — burst mode, then delete everything — is slow, discouraging, and teaches you nothing.

Gapwhy it's open

Camera apps fix the photo after the fact. Pinterest and TikTok posing content is static — you can't ask a screenshot whether you're doing it right. Nothing watches you live, tells you what to change and in which direction, and captures at the right moment. The coaching layer between "here's a pose idea" and "here's your photo" doesn't exist.

Solutionwhat I built

Pick an occasion and vibe; the AI generates target poses. Your webcam runs through pose detection, a skeleton overlay shows alignment, and a vision model scores you against the target — giving one spoken cue at a time ("chin down slightly, drop your left shoulder"). At roughly 85% match the camera fires itself.

AI toolswhat it runs on
  • MediaPipe Pose — real-time landmark detection in the browser
  • GPT-4o Vision — frame analysis, 0–100 scoring, one-priority cues with anti-repetition memory
  • Claude — alternate coaching engine via a local Python proxy
  • Web Speech API — spoken cues
Success metrichow I'd know

Auto-capture rate — the share of started sessions ending in at least one auto-captured photo at ≥85% match. Baseline 0; target 60% within four weeks of launch. It's the one number proving the whole loop works.

01
6-foot UX constraint

Every font, button and truncation judged against a user standing six feet from the screen — which forced 4rem minimum coaching text and repositioned controls.

02
Separated the scoring sources

Vision score exclusively gates auto-capture; skeleton geometry only drives the visual ring. Fixed the core trust breakdown where users executed poses but got no credit.

03
Curated pose library over AI-generated

Replaced the AI's overly complex instructions with 24 named, emoji-anchored poses across 4 style categories — the single largest source of frustrating sessions.

04
Reframed the scoring rubric

GPT-4o was scoring against a studio standard. Rewrote the prompt to score generously for genuine attempts, turning the most-cited frustration into a win state.

05
Coaching memory as a banned list

A session-level JSON banned list passed to every Vision call prevents repeated notes; stuckCount triggers body-part rotation and a user-controlled skip.

Kill signal

If users consistently abandon before their first capture, the coaching isn't landing — that's a rework of the approach, not a backlog of new features.

Engineers and technical ICs presenting to non-technical audiences — sprint reviews, stakeholder demos, leadership updates. The trigger is a high-stakes meeting this week where being misunderstood has a real cost: a delayed decision, a misread status, a missed promotion signal.

Pain pointwhat hurts

Engineers describe work in implementation terms; stakeholders need outcome terms. Jargon goes unexplained, business impact goes unstated, and the actual ask lands in the last sentence.

First-hand observation at Fidelity: scrum masters and business stakeholders genuinely could not parse what engineers were doing. The cost isn't embarrassment — it's slower decisions and quietly broken alignment.

Gapwhy it's open

Poised and Yoodli measure delivery — filler words, pace, energy. Independent comparisons flag the same weakness in both: neither evaluates whether the content works for the audience. Knowing you said "um" twelve times doesn't help when your argument structure is the problem.

Solutionwhat I built

v1 is Jargon Radar: a practice studio where you rehearse and the AI flags unexplained jargon, buried asks, and the missing "so what." After real calls, transcript learnings show where you lost the room.

The core bet, labelled as founder intuition: people who most need communication help won't seek it afterwards — so the coaching attaches to a moment they're already motivated for.

AI toolswhat it runs on
  • Real-time speech-to-text — practice runs and call transcripts
  • Claude / GPT-4o — jargon detection, audience-fit scoring, ask placement, argument structure
  • Speech analysis — tone, pace and clarity under the content analysis
Success metrichow I'd know

Attempt delta — improvement in content-clarity score between a user's first and final practice run before a real meeting. Baseline 0; target: measurable jargon reduction and a clearly stated ask in 70% of multi-attempt sessions.

01
In-call vs post-call feedback

Post-call feedback gets ignored because the emotional context is gone. The hypothesis: coaching delivered mid-call, while the stakes are live, is retained and acted on.

02
Passive listening as the default

Making users trigger analysis breaks the flow of the call. The AI listens continuously and only surfaces a nudge when something actionable appears.

03
Specificity over sentiment

"You sound nervous" is useless. "You said um 6 times in the last 90 seconds" is actionable. Every coaching prompt is written to be concrete rather than evaluative.

Guardrail

Journal and transcript open rate. If users record calls but never open the learnings, the post-call loop is dead weight and gets cut.

Target userwho it's for

Nostalgic, visually creative millennials and Gen Z — the VSCO, Pinterest and Day One crowd. Secondary: gift-givers turning photo collections into something meaningful. The trigger is a trip, a milestone, or a camera-roll scroll that sparks "I should do something with these."

Pain pointwhat hurts

The average person takes 500+ photos a month and almost none become memories. Traditional journaling asks too much: the blank page, the time, the "what do I even write?" In their words: "I love the idea of scrapbooking, I just never actually do it."

Gapwhy it's open

Scrapbooking tools split into two dead ends: too complex (Canva, Photoshop — congratulations, you're a designer now) or too cold (auto-generated photo books with no personality). Neither solves the writing problem, which is the actual blank-page barrier.

Solutionwhat I built

Upload a photo; the AI studies what's in it and generates the whole entry — a poetic title, a two-to-three sentence caption, a handwritten-style memory quote, an evocative date ("a Friday in late September"), and a location, in one of five aesthetic voices. The design layer wraps it in torn edges, aged paper, stamps and washi tape.

AI toolswhat it runs on
  • GPT-4o Vision / Claude Vision — image analysis grounded in what's visible
  • Structured JSON generation — three distinct suggestions per photo, mapped straight to layout
  • Canvas API + CSS art — torn edges, textures, stamps, handwritten type
  • Supabase — the saved journal library across sessions
Success metrichow I'd know

Multi-page sessions — the share of users who make pages for more than one photo in a single session. Baseline 0; target 40% in month one. It's the shift from "trying it" to "building a journal."

Kill signal

If users overwhelmingly make one page and leave, Paperly is a novelty rather than a journal — and needs a rethink before any feature expansion.
